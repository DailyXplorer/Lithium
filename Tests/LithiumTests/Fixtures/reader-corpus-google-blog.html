How we test our batteries before they ship

A look inside the lab where every phone battery is charged, frozen and dropped.

Paragraph 01: before a phone reaches anyone's pocket, its battery spends weeks in a lab that most of the team never sees, being charged and drained thousands of times while machines record every change in its voltage.

Paragraph 02: the first test is the simplest: charge a cell to full, let it rest, and measure how much it loses overnight. A healthy cell loses almost nothing, and one that loses more is pulled for a closer look.

Cold, heat and falls

Paragraph 03: then the chambers take over. Cells are frozen and heated through the range a phone might see in a parked car or on a ski slope, and charged at each step to see how fast they can safely go.

Paragraph 04: the drop rig comes last. Finished phones fall onto steel and concrete from the height of a table, over and over, and the battery inside is scanned afterwards for the smallest dent or swelling.

Paragraph 05: everything the lab learns goes back to the people who design the next phone, which is why the batteries we ship today charge faster and last longer than the ones we shipped three years ago.
