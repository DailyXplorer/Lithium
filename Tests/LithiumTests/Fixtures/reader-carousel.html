Inside a battery gigafactory

Paragraph 01: the building is a kilometre long and almost silent, because most of what happens inside it happens in dry rooms, sealed and kept at a humidity lower than any desert on Earth.

Paragraph 02: from the coating line the foil goes to calendering, where heavy rollers press it to an exact thickness, and then to slitting, where it is cut into ribbons the width of a cell.

Paragraph 03: cells are wound or stacked, filled with electrolyte, sealed, and then charged for the first time, slowly, over days, in racks that fill a hall of their own.

Paragraph 04: that first charge, called formation, builds a thin layer on the anode that decides how long the cell will live, so the plant watches it more closely than anything else.Opens a glossary in a new window

Paragraph 05: a recipe card on the wall lists the mix for the cathode, 1/2 part binder to 3 of 4 parts active material, a ratio the engineers call the house blend and refuse to change.

Paragraph 06: at the end of the line every cell is tested, graded and sorted, and the few that fail go to a recycling plant next door, which grinds them down and sends the metals back to the start; learn more about the plant in our next issue.
