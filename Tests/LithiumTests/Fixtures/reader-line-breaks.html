Paragraph 01: lithium is the lightest of all metals, so light that it floats on water, and on oil too, which is how it is stored in a laboratory, under a layer of mineral oil, away from the air that would tarnish it.

Paragraph 02: it floats because its atoms are small and few in a given space, three protons each and a loose arrangement, which gives it about half the density of water, less than any wood you are likely to own.

Paragraph 03: that same lightness is why batteries use it: a lithium atom gives up its outer electron easily and weighs almost nothing, so more energy fits in every kilogram, which is what phones and cars want.

Paragraph 04: so the next time a phone feels light in the hand, part of the reason is a metal that would float in a glass of water, if only it didn't react with it, fizzing, and catch fire.
